My Empathy Story 🏒

Context

UX designers preach empathy, and though everyone can say they have it, not everyone does. In college, I decided to play hockey. My luck ran out in soccer, so I decided to do something I had never been able to do growing up. I knew what it was like to be on a team from soccer. I knew what it was like to play hockey on our backyard pond. I knew the hockey culture from hanging out with hockey players growing up. But playing on an organized hockey team was completely new to me, and that’s when I gained the most empathy for what it’s like to play hockey.

Problem 1

Despite knowing a decent amount about hockey, I felt like I was in Mexico after a semester of Spanish class. The simple things were difficult for me. Understanding a drill was confusing even though I was very familiar with soccer drills. I found myself at the back of the line observing others doing the drill. If I was lucky, I would piece everything together in time for me to go.

Problem 2

My coach called me out for having roller hockey shin guards and not ice hockey ones. I didn’t know the difference. Just like I don’t know the difference between purple and violet.

Problem 3

I spent time watching YouTube videos on hockey positions. I didn’t even know how to do fluid line changes upon my first game. I had to ask my line mate how the process went while we were already on the bench. Totally out of my element I felt like I was trying to watch the last season of Game of Thrones after only seeing a brief recap of the previous 7.

Problem 4

We played Purdue on my first game, and even though I touched the ice once, I still got yelled at for not coming back to the bench sooner. I was used to playing 90 minutes of soccer, and 30 seconds on the ice was like putting on your running shoes to grab the mail at the end of the driveway.

What I learned

Despite all of this, I learned what it was really like to be a hockey player by becoming one. I learned the difference between waxed and non waxed laces. I learned that white hockey tape was cooler than black tape. I learned that tapping the toe of the blade helps prevent chips. I learned all the different styles of tapping for more control, quicker release, and so on. I learned that coaches really do throw fits in the locker room. I learned that the music and language is extremely vulgar and doesn't exceed an IQ level of 4. I learned why practice jerseys are different colors, and what F1, F2, and F3 meant. I learned what it was like to sit in the penalty box, and what it was like for a teammate to have your back.

Application

I absolutely loved my time playing hockey, and wish I could have played more, but the lessons extend beyond hockey. I learned empathy. I discovered what it was really like to be new to something, and what being a hockey player meant holistically (on and off the ice). I had to lace up my skates to understand what playing hockey is like. UX designers must do likewise to understand their users in an empathetic way.
