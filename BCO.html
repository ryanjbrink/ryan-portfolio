Business Card Organizer

January 2020

Problem

It’s a pain when you forget your business cards whether during or after a networking event. Business Card Organizer solves this problem.

Context

A project with Lambda University. The goal was to better understand the UX process and tools needed to deliver a desirable product.

Objective

Over 4 weeks, I was responsible for the UX design of Business Card Organizer. I gave my research and wireframes to the dev team.

Research

Persona

Accourding to the Problem statement, I put together what a typical user would look like for our product. This individual was highly extroverted, and would frequently visit networking events.

Competitive Research

I researched 6 companies that sought to solve this same issue. I downloaded each app and did some usability testing. I discovered most were difficult to use, and non intuitive. I wanted to create an app that had a great onboarding process, and was easily understood.

Journey Map

I thought about how someone would discover and use the app. What pain points they may have, and how that could be fixed. For example, how annoying would it be if you were trying to connect with someone who did not have the app? Being able to share an e-card with a non app user would be essential.

Interviews

Interviews helped me understand real expectations and wants for a business card app. An intervewee explained that the app could provide certain features that a physical card could not. Such as a link to a relevant case study.

Ideation

Affinity Map

After interviews, I organized concerns and wants through an affinity map. I categorized these trends into similar groupings to see what problems should be solved.

Mind Map

I then did a mind mapping brainstorm session. I thought about all of the features that could solve app problems, and the solutions. I did not hold back and all thoughts were written down.

Crazy 8’s

I did a crazy 8’s excercise. With a minute to sketch one screen eight times, I put my ideas to the paper. Ideating what the screens could actually look like was the starting point to creating wireframes.

User Flows

Once I knew the features I would include in the app, I created user flows which showed how someone would interact with the app doing specific actions. This was the start to my site map.

wireframing

Low Fidelity

My lo-fi rendering was a rough idea of what could be. It depicted the four main screens from the site map.

Mid Fidelity

I had many mid fidelity renderings. Each better than the last. Usability testing helped out significantly here.

  • The copy was confusing in some areas. Card was changed to QR card
  • The calendar tab was changed to the notifications tab where the user would be updated. Home was changed to cards connections instead of the normal feed. The calendar section had been deemed confusing and not important enough to keep. Usability tests showed that users would prefer their own calendars or would refer to their calendars before using the app calendar.
  • Users needed a better way to update their feed, so a call to action was placed above the profile feed to show users where to write.
  • I got rid of the share icon by the name in the profile. Users were confused by the imagery. There are already 3 other ways to share your contact card depending on the user and the situation.

Prototyping & Usability Testing

Prototyping

I used Figma to connect my wireframes together. This allowed users to click through screens on an actual phone using Figma Mirror.

Usability Testing

Users were assigned tasks to complete. With each task they were asked to think out loud. This helped me better understand their thought process when making decisions.

Reiterations

Making Changes

I made changes based on

  • Usability feedback
  • Instructor comments and feedback
  • Further research into competitors and similar apps

With each page and element, I asked myself

  • What is the purpose?
  • Does it provide value?
  • Is it usable?

With any Changes to the Wireframes I also updated userflows, sitemap, and style guide. Whatever had changed needed to also be reflected in the appropriate documents

Hand off to Developers

UX Design Supporting Role

The last week of the project, developers were responsible for creating the app. I was responsible for providing the wireframes, explaining screens and usability, and answering any questions that came up. I organized my files for convenient use of use sub team. A page for the team responisble for creating the landing or marketing page. MVP wireframes, user flows, and site map, and responsive design pages for the react 1 & 2 development teams.

There was one problem with how our team was set up. There were no mobile developers. We used my responsive designs to build a web app for business card organizer. this sufficed for learning, however, a real world application for Business Card Organizer would need a mobile version for networking events.
